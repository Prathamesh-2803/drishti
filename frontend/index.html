The reliability layer ships with every enhanced image: a per-pixel confidence map from the model's own agreement across repeated passes. Bright means it was less certain there. Nothing is invented that the original 10 m pixels don't support — that is enforced by construction, not hoped for.

The ceiling

What real 2.5 m ground truth would unlock.

Today the model is graded the only way possible without a reference: degrade a real 10 m scene, rebuild it, compare. That works, but it rests on one assumption — that the same operator holds from 40 m→10 m as 10 m→2.5 m. Co-registered Indian high-resolution imagery from Bhoonidhi (NRSC) — LISS-IV at 5.8 m, Cartosat-1 PAN at 2.5 m — removes that assumption.

Measure the real task

Validate 10 m → 2.5 m directly against 2.5 m Cartosat, not a simulated stand-in. The headline numbers stop carrying an asterisk.

Fine-tune on Indian ground

Smallholder field sizes, Indian urban form, monsoon-season land cover — a few hundred co-registered pairs adapt the detail the model invents to what is actually there.

Calibrate the confidence

Check the reliability layer against measured error: does "less certain" actually mean "more wrong"? Turn it from a relative map into a calibrated one.

State an effective resolution

Report a measured resolving power — line pairs actually recovered — instead of a nominal ×4, and sharpen the 20 m bands with PAN guidance.

Registration is a solved step (phase correlation / AROSICS) and the fetch, training and evaluation pipeline already accepts real pairs — it is a data access question, not an engineering one.
